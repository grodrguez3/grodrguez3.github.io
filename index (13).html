Research

I build fast, optimized machine learning pipelines that recover tissue mechanical properties directly from data.

My dissertation focuses on building a system that estimates the mechanical properties of the posterior eye. I use physics-informed neural networks to recover those properties directly from deformation data, and convolutional neural networks to segment and quantify the eye's anatomical structure from volumetric OCT scans that feed into the mechanical model.

DeepVFM

Neural discovery of virtual fields for stable inverse constitutive identification

Identifying hyperelastic material parameters usually means running a finite-element model in a loop until the simulation matches the measurement. DeepVFM replaces that loop. A network learns the virtual fields that make the identification well-posed, then solves for the constitutive parameters directly from a noisy displacement field. No ground-truth parameters are needed anywhere in training, because the equilibrium residual is the loss.

RetOCTNet & DeepRetNet

Volumetric OCT segmentation, from research code to a tool people actually use

Custom DenseNet and U-Net/nnU-Net variants for 2D and 3D segmentation of layered tissue in optical coherence tomography volumes, reaching roughly 80% accuracy across preclinical datasets. The models ship as standalone GUI applications, which turned a manual tracing task into something a non-technical researcher runs in an afternoon. Published in TVST.
